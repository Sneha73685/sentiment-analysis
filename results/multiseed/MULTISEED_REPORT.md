# Depression v2 Models — Multi-Seed Stability Experiment

Generated by `experiments/multiseed_depression_v2.py`. Trains a **fresh**
classical model and a **fresh** BERT model per seed on the canonical
`depression_train.csv`, validates/early-stops/fits temperature on
`depression_val.csv` only, and measures once on the frozen
`depression_test.csv`. The canonical seed-42 models
(`depression_model_v2/`, `depression_bert_model_v2/`) and their existing
evaluation/calibration outputs were **not** touched — verified below.

Seeds: **42, 123, 2024, 3407, 7777**. All 5 succeeded for both models —
zero seed failures.

## 1. Mean ± SD across seeds (frozen test set, n=1148 each)

| Metric | Classical | BERT (raw) |
|---|---|---|
| Accuracy | 0.9556 ± 0.0000 | 0.9829 ± 0.0047 |
| F1 | 0.9537 ± 0.0000 | 0.9825 ± 0.0048 |
| ROC-AUC | 0.9900 ± 0.0000 | 0.9975 ± 0.0004 |
| Log-loss | 0.2001 ± 0.0000 | 0.0768 ± 0.0074 |
| Brier | 0.0487 ± 0.0000 | 0.0150 ± 0.0026 |
| ECE | 0.1033 ± 0.0000 | 0.0143 ± 0.0018 |

95% CIs (t-distribution across the 5 seed point-estimates — **not**
bootstrap-over-test-examples CIs; see §8) are in `multiseed_summary.csv`.
For BERT, e.g. accuracy 95% CI = [0.9771, 0.9888]; ECE 95% CI = [0.0121,
0.0166].

**Classical shows exactly zero variance (SD = 0.0000) across all 5
seeds, for every metric.** This is expected and was flagged before
running: `LogisticRegression`'s default solver (`lbfgs`) is deterministic
and does not use `random_state` at all — only `sag`/`saga`/`liblinear` do.
TF-IDF vectorization is likewise deterministic. The five "classical" rows
in `multiseed_results.csv` are therefore bit-for-bit identical, confirmed
directly in the data (not assumed). **This means classical's "stability"
across seeds isn't really a tested hypothesis — the model has no
seed-dependent source of randomness in the first place.** Any true
seed-to-seed classical variability would require either a different
solver (e.g. `saga`) or a randomized data-ordering step, neither of which
is part of the canonical v2 configuration, so this experiment correctly
did not introduce one.

## 2. Is BERT's performance advantage over classical stable across seeds?

**Yes.** BERT's accuracy ranged 0.9774–0.9869 across all 5 seeds — every
single seed beat classical's fixed 0.9556, by a margin of at least 2.2
percentage points (worst BERT seed, 3407/7777) up to 3.1 points (best
seed, 2024). F1 shows the same pattern (BERT range 0.9770–0.9866 vs.
classical's fixed 0.9537). See `accuracy_by_seed.png` / `f1_by_seed.png` —
the two lines never cross or come close to touching.

## 3. Is BERT's calibration advantage stable across seeds?

**Yes, directionally, though with real seed-to-seed spread.** BERT's raw
ECE (0.0125–0.0163) is lower than classical's fixed ECE (0.1033) at every
seed — no seed comes close to erasing this gap. Temperature scaling
further reduces BERT's ECE at every seed (post-calibration range
0.0066–0.0122, always below the corresponding raw value for that seed).
See `ece_by_seed.png`.

## 4. Does BERT remain highly confident on its errors across seeds?

**Yes — this is the most seed-sensitive finding, but the direction never
reverses.** Mean confidence on BERT's errors (raw) ranges from 0.825
(seed 3407) to 0.986 (seed 2024) — real spread (SD 0.071 across seeds),
but **every single seed's value sits far above classical's fixed 0.650**,
and 4 of 5 seeds are above 0.94. Temperature scaling reduces this
somewhat at every seed (calibrated range 0.787–0.931) but never brings it
close to classical's level. See `error_confidence_by_seed.png` — the
BERT lines dip at seed 3407 (also BERT's worst accuracy seed, and its
seed with the most errors, n=25) but never cross below classical's flat
line. **Conclusion: BERT-overconfident-on-errors, identified in the
single-seed error/calibration analyses, is a stable property of this
architecture+data combination, not a seed-42 artifact — though its
severity varies meaningfully by seed.**

## 5. Range of learned temperatures

T ranged **1.433 (seed 3407) to 2.069 (seed 2024)**, mean 1.708 ± 0.242
(SD). All 5 values are comfortably above 1.0, consistent with BERT being
directionally overconfident at every seed (T>1 is the correction direction
for overconfidence). No seed required T<1 (which would indicate
underconfidence instead).

## 6. Seeds that behave unusually

**Seed 3407** stands out on two fronts simultaneously: lowest BERT
accuracy (0.9782) and F1 (0.9775) of the five seeds, highest BERT error
count (25, vs. 15–26 range — actually tied-highest with seed 7777's 26),
and lowest learned temperature (1.433) alongside the lowest mean
confidence-on-errors (0.825 raw / 0.787 calibrated). This is internally
consistent — a seed whose BERT model is both less accurate and (correctly)
less overconfident on the errors it does make — rather than contradictory,
so it doesn't look like a training failure, just the seed with the
weakest random initialization draw for this particular fine-tune. **Seed
7777** has the highest error count (26) but did *not* have unusually low
confidence-on-errors (0.874 raw) — worth noting these two "worst
accuracy" seeds (3407, 7777) don't share the same calibration signature.
No seed showed signs of a genuinely failed or degenerate training run
(e.g. accuracy near chance, NaN losses, non-convergent temperature fit).

## 8. Methodological limitations

1. **n=5 seeds** — the t-distribution 95% CIs on the cross-seed mean are
   wide with only 5 samples (e.g. BERT accuracy CI spans nearly a full
   percentage point). These CIs describe *seed-to-seed* variability and
   are explicitly **not** the bootstrap-over-test-examples CIs reported in
   the earlier Phase-5 calibration analysis (`results/calibration/`) —
   the two answer different questions (how much would results change with
   a different random initialization/training run, vs. how much would
   results change with a different sample of test examples from the same
   population) and should not be conflated or combined.
2. **Classical's zero variance means it contributes no information about
   classical-model training stability** — only about whether the fixed,
   deterministic classical fit reliably beats/loses-to BERT. A genuine
   classical-stability experiment would need to vary something that
   actually randomizes the fit (e.g. bootstrap-resampled training data, or
   solver='saga').
3. **BERT's per-seed error counts are still small** (15–26 out of 1148),
   so per-seed calibration statistics (especially confidence-on-errors)
   carry real sampling noise on top of genuine seed-to-seed variation;
   the two are not separated in this analysis.
4. **Temperature was refit per seed on that seed's own validation
   predictions** (not reused from the canonical seed-42 temperature) —
   this is the methodologically correct choice (each seed's logits have
   their own scale) but means the reported T values reflect 5 independent
   fits, not 5 measurements of one "true" temperature.
5. **No architecture or hyperparameter variation was tested** — this
   experiment isolates *training-seed* stability only. It says nothing
   about robustness to different hyperparameters, data splits, or model
   architectures.
6. **Same caveat as prior phases applies**: none of this is a claim about
   clinical validity — it measures model-vs-model and seed-vs-seed
   agreement on a fixed benchmark, nothing about real-world diagnostic
   performance.
